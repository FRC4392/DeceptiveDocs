## Preseason

Preseason is everything before kickoff. It is the calmest part of the year and the best time to learn something new, because nothing is due yet.

What happens during preseason:

- New members join, get oriented, and try different disciplines.
- Returning members train new members on tools, software, and machines.
- Safety training and machine sign-offs happen so you are cleared to work once build season starts.
- Fundraising and sponsor letters go out. The team needs to raise money before the season, not during it.
- Outreach events introduce STEM to our community.
- The shop gets cleaned, sorted, and stocked.
- Tryouts and selections for the special competition roles happen in this window.

## Kickoff

**Kickoff** is one day in early January when *FIRST* releases the new game to every team in the world at the same moment. Nobody, including the mentors, knows the game before that morning.

What happens on kickoff day:

1. We watch the game reveal video together.
2. We read the game manual, which is the rulebook for the season.
3. We break down the game into scoring actions and figure out what a robot has to be able to do.
4. We start listing ideas. We do not start building.

## Build season

**Build season** is the six to seven weeks after kickoff when the robot gets designed, made, wired, and programmed. It is the busiest part of the year and the schedule above is at its heaviest.

Build season roughly follows this order. The phases overlap, and the whole robot is never built in one straight line.

| Week | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Game analysis, strategy, and prototyping. Test ideas quickly and cheaply. |
| Week 2 | Design decisions locked. CAD begins in earnest. |
| Weeks 3 – 4 | Manufacturing. Parts get made and subsystems get assembled. |
| Week 5 | Electrical work and first robot assembly. Software gets on the real robot. |
| Week 6 | Programming, autonomous routines, and driver practice. |
| Final week | Bumpers, spares, pit setup, packing, and practice. |

:::note[Why the last week matters]
The last week of build season is not extra time to add mechanisms. It is time for the drive team to practice, for spare parts to get made, and for the pit to get packed. A robot that works and has a practiced driver beats a robot that is more complicated and untested.
:::

## Offseason

Offseason is everything after our last competition. It is short, and it is where next season gets easier or harder.

What happens during offseason:

- The robot is torn down, and parts, tools, and inventory get returned and recorded.
- We reflect on the season: what worked, what did not, and what to change.
- Documentation gets written and updated while the season is still fresh. See [Contributing to the Docs](/docs/start-here/contributing).
- We attend an offseason event. These are unofficial, usually one day, and are a low-pressure way for new members to see a competition and for new drivers to get practice.
- Awards submissions, media, and sponsor thank-yous get finished.
- Training for next season begins, which loops back to preseason.
