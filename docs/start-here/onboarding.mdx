# New Member Onboarding

Welcome to Deceivers Robotics. This page tells you what happens next, what our expectations are, and where to find everything you need.

## What Deceivers Robotics is

Deceivers Robotics is team 4392 in the *FIRST* Robotics Competition team, founded in 2012.

***FIRST*** stands for *For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology*. It is the organization that runs the competition. We compete as part of the ***FIRST* in Michigan ** district. They are the local Program Delivery Partner who oversees *FIRST* throughout the state of Michigan. ***FIRST* Robotics Competition**, usually shortened to **FRC**, is the high school competition.

*FIRST* is more than robots. Outside of building the robot, we have to fundraise and gain sponsors to keep the team sustainable. We also spread STEM throughout our community in outreach events. Our outreach helps us compete for the *FIRST* Impact Award.

What to expect in the year

- Ending the previous season and starting the new season we usually attend an offseason event. This is an unofficial, and often short competition that makes a a great introduction to the program
- In early January, *FIRST* releases a brand new game. That day is called **kickoff**.
- We then have to design, manufacture, and program a robot to complete the game objective. This period is usually referred to as **build season.**
- Around 7 or so weeks later competitions will begin. We will attend between 2 and 4 competitions depending on if we advance to the state championship and world championship.
- The rest of the year, called **offseason**, we will train, fundraise, do outreach, and get ready to do it again.

## Joining the team

You and your parents will be required to:
- sign the [Student Handbook](/docs/start-here/handbook) before attending team meetings. A parent meeting will be announced where everyone will be introduced to the handbook.
- make an account and sign consent and release forms with *FIRST*. This must be completed before attending kickoff.

You will also need to create an account with our team to access all our internal tools.

What to do during your first meeting:

1. Come dressed to be around tools:
	- Closed-toe and heel shoes, not flip flops, sandals, crocs
	- Dangling jewelry or hoodie cords should be removed or tucked away.
	- We provide eye protection. You will wear it any time a machine or robot is running.
2. Read this page
3. Get started

## How to get started

**Step 1 - get oriented.** Learn the shop safety rules. Get to know the mentors and the students. Join our Discord server. Find out where the bathroom, the tools, and the trash are.

**Step 2 - try things.** Spend time trying different disciplines. Ask a student or mentor to show you how things work, or how things are done.

**Step 3 - help on a task.** Work with another student or mentor on a task. Try to learn as you help.

**Step 3 - take on a full task.** Ask for tasks that fit your knowledge level and own it, but still don't be afraid to ask for help.

Two habits you should have:

- **Sign in and out every day.** See the [Hours](/docs/start-here/team-tools/hours) page.
- **Ask early.** Sitting confused is worse than asking a question you think is dumb. There is no such thing as a dumb question.

## Important Terms

### *FIRST* Impact Award*

The FIRST Impact Award is the most prestigious award in FIRST Robotics Competition. It honors the team that best represents a model for other teams to emulate and best embodies the mission of *FIRST*. It is awarded based on how a team spreads FIRST's mission, the impact the team has on the *FIRST* community, and how the team inspires the future of STEM.

### Gracious Professionalism

Gracious Professionalism is a term coined by Dr. Woodie Flowers, one of the founders of *FIRST*. It's a way of doing things that encourages high quality work, emphasizes the value of others, and respects individuals and the community. With Gracious Professionalism, fierce competition and mutual gain can be achieved together.

### Coopertition

Coopertition involves kindness and respect even in the heat of competition. It means cooperating with each other even as you are competing.

## Other words you will hear

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |,
| kickoff | The January day FIRST releases the new game. |
| build season | The weeks after kickoff where the robot gets built |
| match | One round of the game, about two and a half minutes long. |
| alliance | The three teams that play together on the red or blue side of a match. Alliances change every match during qualifications. |
| autonomous | The first part of a match, when the robot runs on code alone with no driver input. |
| teleop | The rest of the match after autonomous, when the robot can be controlled by human drivers. |

| drive team | The small group allowed on the field during a match. |
| scouting | Watching and recording how other robots perform so we can make good strategy and alliance choices. |
| mentor | Adults who help the team |
| SystemCore | The robot's main computer. |
| Driver Station | The laptop and software the drive team uses to control the robot. |

## What we expect from you

- Attend meetings and be productive. You will get out of the team what you put into the team.
- Follow the rules. The rules are there to protect you and to keep everyone safe.
- Learn new things. Try something you haven't tried before.
- Have fun. Enjoy meetings and competitions.
- Practice Gracious Professionalism.

## What you can expect from us

The mentors are here to support you and help you learn. We will assist you whenever you need help, give you feedback to help you improve, and we will challenge you when we know you can handle it. We are all part of the same team and we all want to see the team succeed. Mentors are also not perfect and we will listen to any feedback you give us.

## Who to ask questions

Don't be afraid to ask questions. Mentors will always be able to either answer you questions or direct you to the correct person to ask.
